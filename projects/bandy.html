02

Starting point

Music for people who don’t read music.

Most ways into music start with rules: notes, scales and practice. We wanted a way in that starts with play, where nothing can be “wrong” and anyone can make a band in seconds. That led to three principles.

  1. Touch, don’t readEvery musical choice is a physical block you can hold and see.
  2. Every combination worksBlocks are tuned to sound good together, so mistakes become surprises.
  3. Characters, not controlsA face reads as a mood long before it reads as a rhythm setting.
03

How to play

Stack up. Line up. Pick a scene.

Vertical: one memberHat = effect · Face = rhythm · Outfit = instrument

Horizontal: the bandEach member adds a layer. Up to six on the base.

Scene board: the genreRead by NFC, it sets the base rhythm and melody.

  1. Hat

    Sound effect

    Monster, elf, electric, glitch, bubble or robot: the hat colours the voice.

  2. Face

    Rhythm

    An angry face hits hard; a sleepy one drags lazily. The mood sets the beat.

  3. Outfit

    Instrument

    Guitar, singer, piano, bass, violin or drums: the outfit decides who plays.

04

Play the band

Your turn: put a band on the board.

Turn your sound on

Every note is made live in your browser. Speakers or headphones work best.

Scene board

Left to right is time: the six columns play in turn and loop. Members in the same column play together. Rows are pitch: the back row plays high, the front row low. Swap a block: drag one from the tray onto a member, or tap a block, then a new one. Move a member: drag it to another spot.

05

Scene boards

Same band, a different genre.

The board behind the band sets the genre. An NFC tag inside it tells the base which scene is in, and the same piano player sounds completely different at a jazz club than on a rock stage. Our prototype has the country board; try the others in the band above.

The real prototype: a laser-cut base with tenon-and-mortise joints and a hand-painted, layered country board.

06

How it works

Look inside.

A resistor in every block

Each block hides a resistor with its own value. One leg goes to a shared ground, the other to a corner pin that maps to its layer: pin 32 for the instrument, 33 for the effect, 34 for the rhythm.

Pogo pins

Spring-loaded pins on the top and bottom faces are wired through, so three blocks are read at once just by stacking.

Inside the base

The ESP32 reads the three voltages, matches them to calibrated ranges and sends the combo, like “bass,laughing,elf”, to Processing, which loops the right sound. In our MVP the laptop plays it; the speaker and NFC reader are part of the final design.

Scene board

An NFC tag in each board tells the base which genre is in, so it can set the base rhythm and melody the band plays over.

Tap a hotspot, or let it walk you through.

A working MVP

Before painting anything, we proved the core loop: stack blocks on the breadboard rig, and the laptop recognises them and plays the matching sound in real time, with a debounce so a wobbly contact doesn’t cut the music.
