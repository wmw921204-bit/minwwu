UX RESEARCH · INDUSTRY PARTNERSHIP

One login and an active dashboard for FINRA.

FINRA’s users juggle many systems, logins and forms, and often can’t tell where to go next. With FINRA, our SCADpro team designed one login and an active dashboard that guide firms from sign-in to finished filings.

01

Entering the maze

When users feel uncertain, they don’t search harder. They leave.

Our goal: create a unified login and dashboard that streamlines navigation, task completion and permission-based access across FINRA’s platforms. If FINRA’s systems are inefficient, firms struggle to finish required tasks on time, raising the risk of mistakes and compliance issues.

62.2%of FINRA.org visitors leave after viewing one page
30%of FINRA’s call volume is about logging in
$100Mspent each year by securities firms searching for information
02

The first turns: research

Users say “FINRA” for a whole constellation of systems.

We combined SME interviews and user surveys with forum research, case studies of high-trust platforms, and AI models. Gateway, FinPro, Web CRD, the DR Portal: users meet many entry points without understanding how they fit together.

  • “I can’t log into FINRA.”
  • “How to deal with it or whom do I connect with… Can anyone help or share the process?”
  • “Should I be worried?”
  • “Is there a support email?”

Real posts from Reddit and other forums. Unsure about contacting their regulator, users turn to anonymous “shadow support” for help.

SME interviews

Expert bias

Internal staff had made decisions for users without enough direct research. Experts pointed us to a “lobby” concept and an active workspace, built for firms of very different sizes.

Emotional journey

Exam passed, then a wall

A user passes a major career milestone, then can’t log in. With no feedback from the system, anxiety builds and they start doubting their own success.

Case studies

Trust comes from clarity

High-trust financial platforms orient users right after login, reveal complexity progressively, and deliberately slow down high-risk actions.

03

Wandering the maze: synthesis

700 data points, six problems.

700+data points
110groups
27themes
6problems
  1. Confusing navigation and multiple entry points
  2. High cognitive load
  3. Trust and security
  4. Tasks fragmented across systems
  5. Redundant, inconsistent interface design
  6. Locating, dividing and resuming tasks
04

Exploring the maze: iterations

From 85 sketches to a connected system.

We sketched over 85 ideas, then built mid-fidelity login and dashboard wireframes in three rounds.

Usability testing

18 participants aged 18–40 tried five realistic tasks: log in with a firm account, use AI to learn Form U4, find the submission portal, read notifications, and switch accounts.

100%task completion across all five tasks
18participants, plus an A/B visual variant
  • Trust drops before usability does.
  • AI created the most cognitive friction, despite full task success.
  • Users hesitate when interaction cues are invisible.
  • Account switching failed at feedback, not functionality.
The pivot

At Week 7, FINRA pushed us to bring FINRA.org content into the task-based workflow. We shifted from building “just another login” to a connected system that bridges workflow and knowledge, scalable across FinPro, Gateway and the rest.

05

Finding the exit: try it

Final prototype · coded

01 / 12

Start on FINRA.org

Most people begin on FINRA.org, not in a dashboard. So does our flow: this is the Form U4 page, with our additions layered on top.

TryOpen the chat bubble in the bottom-right corner.

A universal chatbot

To unify FINRA.org with the dashboard, a chatbot is always there as a familiar sign of reliable help. When a task needs your account, it hands you straight to login.

TryClose the chat and press Log In at the top.

One secure login

One entrance for every FINRA system, with username, email or Google. It replaces the many separate logins users kept confusing.

TryType any email address, such as demo@example.com. Nothing is sent or stored.

Verify your identity

A one-time passcode goes to the user’s preferred contact, so the single login stays secure.

TryEnter any four digits, then press Enter.

Onboarding

A short tour points out every key feature, so the new dashboard never feels like another maze.

TryClick Next, or close the tour.

An active workspace

What to address first comes first: due soon, in progress and updates. Filings show their progress, and pending access requests are surfaced right away.

TryTry the bell, the widgets icon or Switch Account.

Staying up to date

Notifications keep users informed of important updates. Urgent tasks are emphasised, so they get handled promptly.

TryFilter by Personal or Firm-Wide.

A personalised dashboard

Users choose what they see by adding or removing widgets, and the system suggests widgets based on their activity.

TryPick a widget to add.

Switch accounts

Many users act for several firms. They can find and switch accounts with a verification code instead of logging in again.

TrySearch or sort the accounts.

The AI assistant

Ask in plain language instead of hunting through menus. Chats can be revisited and pinned in the chat history.

TryAsk how to fill out a Form U4.

Answers that lead to action

The assistant explains the steps, then offers to start the filing right away, connecting knowledge and workflow.

TryPress Start New Filing.

Start a filing

Long filings can be saved as drafts, so work survives across sessions instead of being lost to a timeout.

TrySave a draft, or go back.

Request access, simply

FINRA’s complex entitlement process becomes one request, sent to the right admin.

Details on demand

Pop-ups stay short; a details panel opens more context only when it is needed.

No surprise redirects

When a task lives on another FINRA site, a pop-up says so before taking you there.

Take a break

Break Mode blurs the dashboard without logging out. A code brings you back, fixing the aggressive sign-outs that lost users’ work.

TryRe-enter the dashboard with any code.

Signing out

Leaving is always a deliberate, confirmed step.

Settings and control

For a greater sense of control and security, users choose their preferences, including whether to use AI tools.

06

Client feedback

“Connected the dots with storytelling.”

We presented the process as a journey through a maze, from entering to finding the exit, then let the FINRA team explore the coded prototype themselves.

What they loved

From website pain to the gateway

The design connected the issues users have on today’s website to what they see in the gateway, and the sidebar made the platform more action-oriented.

Looking forward

Earn trust in AI

Move the chatbot into the sidebar so it never blocks content, make it clear when AI mode is on with an on/off toggle, and find a better way to show progress than exact percentages.
