01

The problem

Panic happens fast.
Support should be faster.

1/9

college students have had at least one unexpected panic attack.

Telch et al., 1989 · n = 2,375
2×

the risk of a panic disorder diagnosis in autistic adults.

Nimmo-Smith et al., 2020

How could support be easier to reach in exactly that moment?

02

Emotional journey

Pause, reconnect, re-enter.

Instead of asking a panicking student to make choices, Moment follows the body back up, one sensory step at a time.

03

Inside the lens

Twelve screens, one instruction each.

A mind in panic can’t read a paragraph. Every screen carries one short line, one key word underlined, and one shape to follow. Scroll through the whole reset:

  1. A pinch on the Neural Band starts a three-minute reset.

  2. Motion sensing notices you are still standing, and asks you to sit first.

  3. Weight goes into the chair, and the body learns it is safe.

  4. The ring grows and shrinks to pace each breath.

  5. Each bead is one slow breath. The swipe gives the hands a rhythm too.

  6. Touch pulls attention back into the body fastest.

  7. The camera takes one snapshot of the room, with your permission.

  8. MOMO picks one real object to anchor you. No names, no faces.

  9. A warm cup in your hand becomes the next grounding step.

  10. Hearing brings back the room beyond the object.

  11. Widen the view again, at your own pace.

  12. Return when you are ready. Pinch to exit.

04

How it works

Four quiet signals, no screen to unlock.

Nobody nearby should notice. Support starts with a word or a pinch, and the glasses do the sensing.

  1. 01

    Voice activation

    One voice command starts the routine, so there are fewer steps between needing support and getting it.

  2. 02

    Neural Band gestures

    Pinch to start or exit, swipe to spin the ring. Small finger moves that nobody else sees.

  3. 03

    Motion sensing

    Built-in sensors read movement and steadiness, and prompt you to sit down before continuing.

  4. 04

    Camera + AI

    One snapshot of the room turns nearby objects and colours into grounding prompts.

05

Privacy by design

Come back to the room, not to the people in it.

A camera on your face is a lot to ask. So the grounding step takes as little as it can, and lets go of it straight away.

  1. 1 snapshot
    01 · Asks first

    Only inside a reset you started

    The camera looks once, and only with your permission.

  2. 02 · Objects only

    A thing, never a person

    MOMO looks for one object or colour. No faces, no names.

  3. Notice the white item nearby.
    03 · One prompt

    Turned into a single line

    All that survives is the instruction in your lens.

  4. 04 · Nothing kept

    Then the snapshot is gone

    No photo saved, no log of where you were.

06

Make it yours

Decide on a calm day, not in the moment.

The companion app is where the choices live: which channels to use (glasses display, voice, phone vibration, a slow heartbeat), and which breathing pattern feels right. Then, mid-panic, there is nothing left to decide.
